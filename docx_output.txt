PATIENT-SOVEREIGNMEDICAL TRUST NETWORK
Detailed Product Workflow & Hackathon Implementation Scenario
Patient-controlled access • Cryptographic consent • AI prescription safety • Fraud detection
1. Product Concept
The platform is a patient-controlled healthcare trust network. The patient is the final authority over access to their medical records. Doctors and hospitals use a shared clinical dashboard; pharmacies and insurance companies use a shared verification/claims dashboard. Every important transaction is authenticated, digitally signed where appropriate, and recorded in an auditable trail.

2. Three Dashboard Structure
2.1 Patient Dashboard
Health summary: medical records, prescriptions, active medicines, lab reports and allergies.
Access Requests: incoming requests from doctors, hospitals, pharmacies and insurers.
Consent Management: approve, reject, view, revoke and monitor access.
Prescriptions: view signed prescriptions and AI safety explanations.
Activity/Audit Timeline: every important access, prescription, verification and security event.
Security Alerts: unauthorized access, invalid signatures, suspicious transactions and fraud alerts.
Primary action: 'Who can access my data?'
2.2 Doctor / Hospital Dashboard
Shared dashboard for doctors and hospital staff with role-based permissions.
Search patients and request only the required data fields.
View records only after valid patient consent.
Create prescriptions and run AI safety checks.
Digitally sign prescriptions.
Review hospital-level access and security activity.
2.3 Pharmacy / Insurance Dashboard
Shared business dashboard with role-based permissions.
Pharmacy: verify prescriptions, check signature, expiry and redemption status.
Pharmacy: record dispensing transactions.
Insurance: verify prescription, dispensing and claim information.
Insurance: submit/review claims and detect quantity/amount inconsistencies.
Monitor fraud and anomaly alerts.
3. Complete Product Workflow — Beginning to End
Step 1 — Patient Registration
The patient creates an account with basic identity information. The system creates a unique Patient ID and establishes the cryptographic identity used for consent and transaction verification.
Patient  ↓Account creation  ↓Patient ID  ↓Secure cryptographic identity  ↓Patient Health Vault
Step 2 — Medical Records Enter the Health Vault
Records can originate from a hospital, doctor, diagnostic provider or patient upload. Important records are assigned a Record ID, issuer identity, timestamp, hash and digital signature/provenance metadata.
Medical Record  ├── Diagnosis  ├── Prescription  ├── Lab Report  └── Allergy Information        ↓Record ID + Timestamp + Hash + Issuer
Step 3 — Doctor/Hospital Requests Access
The doctor does not automatically see the patient's complete medical history. The doctor selects the minimum fields required and provides a mandatory purpose and access duration.
REQUEST ACCESSRequester: Dr. Arun / ABC HospitalData:  ☑ Current medications  ☑ Allergies  ☑ Previous prescriptions  ☐ Complete historyPurpose: Clinical consultationDuration: 4 hours        ↓SEND REQUEST
Step 4 — Request Appears on Patient Dashboard
The patient receives a clear request showing who wants access, exactly which data is requested, the reason, and how long the access will remain valid.
ABC HOSPITALDr. Arun KumarWants:✓ Current medications✓ Allergies✓ Previous prescriptionsPurpose: Prescription consultationDuration: 4 hours[REJECT]   [APPROVE & SIGN]
Step 5 — Patient Approves or Rejects
The patient can reject the request immediately. If approved, the patient authenticates the action and creates a cryptographically verifiable consent.
Reject → request closedApprove → authentication → digital signature → consent creation
Step 6 — QR + Phone Biometric Fallback
If the laptop has no fingerprint sensor, the web app generates a short-lived, one-time QR. The patient scans it with the registered phone. The phone displays the exact request and performs local fingerprint, face or PIN verification. The biometric itself is not sent to the server; the platform receives a cryptographic proof from the authentication mechanism.
Laptop  ↓One-time QR  ↓Registered Patient Phone  ↓Show exact request  ↓Fingerprint / Face / PIN  ↓Cryptographic proof  ↓Server verification
Step 7 — Consent Record Is Created
After successful authentication, the system creates a scoped consent containing patient, requester, requested fields, purpose, expiry, timestamp, consent ID and signature.

Step 8 — Doctor Gets Only Authorized Information
The doctor dashboard displays only the data covered by the active consent. Unrequested insurance information or unrelated records remain inaccessible.

Step 9 — Doctor Creates Prescription
The doctor enters drug, dose, frequency, duration, route and clinical reason. The prescription is sent to the AI clinical safety engine before final signing.

Step 10 — AI Prescription Safety Analysis
The AI/rule-based clinical engine compares the new prescription against the patient's authorized clinical context and a drug knowledge base. It checks allergies, drug-drug interactions, contraindications, dosage and relevant history.

The system returns a risk score and explains the factors behind it. The AI should advise rather than become the final clinical authority.

The doctor can modify the prescription or override the AI recommendation with a mandatory clinical justification. The override is itself recorded and signed.

The finalized prescription receives a cryptographic signature and integrity hash so later modification can be detected.

4. Fraud Detection — Detailed Explanation
Fraud detection in this problem statement should be interpreted broadly: the platform should identify suspicious medical-data and prescription transactions, tampering, unauthorized access, abnormal identity behavior, duplicate prescriptions and suspicious insurance claims. The system should flag anomalies for review rather than automatically accuse a person of fraud.
4.1 Prescription Duplication
Detect repeated or highly similar prescriptions for the same patient within an unusually short time window.
Patient P10009:15 → Doctor A → Drug X → 30 units09:32 → Doctor B → Drug X → 30 units10:05 → Doctor C → Drug X → 30 unitsSignal:Same patient + same/similar drug + multiple providers + short interval→ Suspicious prescription pattern
4.2 Prescription Tampering
Cryptographic signatures and hashes detect modification after a prescription was signed.
Original:Medicine X, 500 mgHash: A83F...Modified:Medicine X, 1000 mgHash: 92BC...Signature verification → INVALID→ Prescription integrity failure→ Block / alert
4.3 Unauthorized Record Access
Block requests where there is no valid patient consent, the consent has expired, requested scope exceeds consent, or the request signature is invalid.
Requester: Unknown HospitalRequested: Complete medical historyPatient consent: NOT FOUNDSignature: INVALID→ ACCESS BLOCKED→ Patient notified
4.4 Stolen / Abnormal Doctor Identity
Detect unusual combinations such as a new device, unusual time, unusual location and abnormal prescription volume. These are risk signals, not proof of wrongdoing.
Doctor DR102Normal: Chennai, 08:00–18:00Observed: Delhi, 03:14 AMNew deviceAbnormally high activity→ Identity anomaly→ High risk→ Verification required
4.5 Pharmacy / Insurance Claim Anomaly
Compare signed prescription quantity, pharmacy dispensing quantity and insurance claim quantity/amount.
Prescription: 10 unitsDispensed: 10 unitsInsurance claim: 100 units→ Quantity mismatch→ Claim anomaly→ Manual review
4.6 Prescription Replay / Duplicate Redemption
Prevent a valid prescription from being reused after it has already been dispensed.
RX99281Pharmacy A → already redeemedPharmacy B → tries RX99281 again→ Already used→ Replay / duplicate transaction detected→ Block

6. Recommended Fraud Detection Approach
Layer 1 — deterministic rules for hard security failures: invalid signature, missing consent, expired consent, prescription already redeemed, scope mismatch and quantity mismatch.
Layer 2 — behavioral anomaly detection for unusual activity: access frequency, transaction frequency, device changes, location/time anomalies and unusual prescription patterns.
Isolation Forest is a practical hackathon model for unsupervised anomaly detection because it does not require a huge labeled fraud dataset.
Output should include risk score, detected signals, evidence and recommended action.
Use terms such as 'suspicious activity', 'anomaly' and 'requires review' rather than claiming confirmed fraud.

8. Security and Cryptographic Model
Do not store raw fingerprints on the server.
Use phone/device biometric authentication through a secure mechanism such as WebAuthn/passkeys where feasible.
The QR should contain only a short-lived request reference/nonce, not medical data.
Use a one-time QR with a short expiry, for example 60 seconds.
Store medical data off-chain/off-ledger; use hashes, signatures, consent records and an append-only audit layer for integrity/provenance.
Each access should be scoped by requester, requested fields, purpose and expiry.
Every security-sensitive action should create an audit event.

10. Final Product Positioning / USP
A patient-controlled healthcare network where doctors and hospitals request only the medical data they need, patients authorize access through cryptographic/phone-based authentication, AI evaluates prescriptions against the patient's clinical context, pharmacies and insurers verify signed transactions, and an intelligent fraud engine continuously detects tampering, unauthorized access, duplicate prescriptions and suspicious claims.
Killer line for judges: "The patient doesn't just own their medical data — they can prove who accessed it, why they accessed it, what they accessed, and whether anything suspicious happened."
